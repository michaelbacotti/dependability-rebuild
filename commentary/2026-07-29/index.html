Morning Update: Oil Spikes +2.5% on Iran Escalation; Nasdaq Lags as FOMC Day Arrives

WTI crude surges +4.82% to $83.08 on renewed Iran Hormuz concern — largest spike in weeks. Nasdaq lags on Micron -8.89%. S&P futures hold steady as FOMC day arrives.

Morning Market Analysis — Wednesday, July 29, 2026

The market's pre-FOMC calm broke overnight with a sharp crude oil spike that is reframing the session's dominant narrative. West Texas Intermediate surged to $83.08 per barrel — up +4.82% — in the most significant single-session oil move in several weeks, driven by renewed concern over Strait of Hormuz access as the Iran pause that held through the weekend shows signs of fraying. That energy shock is creating a bifurcated tape: S&P futures hold modestly positive as energy equities absorb the bid, while the Nasdaq lags sharply — Micron (MU) is down -8.89% in pre-market trading with volume already exceeding 2.3 million shares, the largest single-session pressure on the stock in months. The FOMC's 2:00 PM ET rate decision and 2:30 PM ET Powell press conference remain the central event of the day, but the oil read-through is adding a supply-shock dimension to whatever Powell says about the inflation and growth outlook.

Overnight Headlines

WTI Crude Surges +4.82% to $83.08 — Largest Single-Day Spike in Weeks: Oil markets snapped a six-session relative calm overnight as reports surfaced suggesting the diplomatic pause around Strait of Hormuz access is under renewed pressure. WTI moved from an open of $80.04 to an intraday high of $83.30 before settling at $83.08 as of 6:49 AM ET — a +$3.47 move from Tuesday's $79.26 open and well above the $82.61 close area that had been the reference level. Brent crude is implied in the $90-92 area. The move is the most acute oil price spike since the initial Hormuz tension escalation in mid-July. Energy equities (XLE) are up sharply in pre-market — the ETF print at $58.55 versus Tuesday's $57.72 close implies a +1.44% energy sector open, the likely best sector of the day. The key question for Powell's 2:30 PM ET press conference: does he acknowledge the oil supply shock as a fresh inflation risk, or frame it as a transitory energy market event?

Nasdaq Lags as Micron Sells Off -8.89%: The Semi-Sector Headline Is Real: Micron (MU) is the single most-concentrated pre-market mover, down -8.89% to $814.85 on volume of 2.4 million shares as of 7:04 AM ET. That volume already exceeds the 52-week average and suggests institutional distribution. The Nasdaq 100 futures contract (NQ) is down -0.88% to 27,943.25 in response — a 247-point deficit versus a flat-to-positive S&P — as the chip sector's weight in the Nasdaq creates a disproportionate drag. The XLK (Technology Select Sector SPDR) print at $171.12 is essentially flat versus Tuesday's $171.16 close, suggesting the broad tech sector is not uniformly selling off — the pressure is concentrated in semis, not software or internet names. Intel (INTC) is down -5.86% to $86.30 on its own catalyst. NVDA is holding +0.35% at $197.20, suggesting the AI infrastructure thesis is not universally impaired. Watch whether the chip-specific weakness extends to the broader Nasdaq by the open.

FOMC Arrives at a More Complex Moment Than Expected — Decision 2:00 PM ET, Powell 2:30 PM ET: Tuesday's pre-FOMC defensive rotation (healthcare +2.47%, staples +1.84%) was orderly and contained — textbook positioning ahead of a known binary catalyst. But the overnight oil shock has added a third input to Powell's press conference that was not present 24 hours ago: a supply-side energy price spike that could push gasoline prices higher in the August driving season and complicate the disinflation narrative. The consensus view entering this week was that the Fed holds at 4.25-4.50% and Powell signals the conditions for a September cut are intact. That view is still likely correct, but the oil tape adds asymmetry: a Powell that acknowledges oil as a transitory supply shock and confirms the labor market is the primary focus should be market-supportive; a Powell that frames the oil move as a reason to "wait and see" would be read as hawkish and would pressure equities broadly while supporting the dollar. No updated dot plot at this meeting — the next SEP release is September 15-16.

The Structural Read

The interplay between the oil supply shock and today's FOMC decision creates a more nuanced policy dilemma than the market anticipated entering the week. Two weeks ago, the thesis was straightforward: disinflation confirmed, labor market resilient, oil prices easing, September cut fully priced. The Iran escalation premium re-entering the market — WTI at $83.08 versus $79.26 just two sessions ago — adds a supply-side inflation variable that complicates Powell's "transitory energy" framing. The structural bull case for equities remains intact: disinflation is the dominant trend, not a transient fluctuation, and the long-term AI capex cycle and Q2 earnings strength in financials, industrials, and consumer staples confirm the fundamental backdrop. But the oil move introduces near-term volatility as the market recalibrates what Powell can credibly say about the inflation path when gasoline prices are moving higher in August.

The Nasdaq's pre-market lag — NQ -0.88% versus ES +0.50% — is the most tactically important signal heading into the open. The concentration of selling in Micron (-8.89%) and Intel (-5.86%) suggests the market is making a specific call on semiconductor valuations and AI infrastructure spending timelines, not rejecting the broader tech thesis. NVDA holding +0.35% is the critical confirmation: if AI infrastructure leaders like NVDA and AVGO remain bid while semis-specific names like MU sell off, the Nasdaq's -0.88% futures deficit is a sector rotation, not a capitulation event. The structural read: the energy-led pre-FOMC session is positioning normalization, not a thesis break. The 7,350-7,500 range is the operational zone; Powell's 2:30 PM ET press conference is the binary event that determines whether the market uses the energy bid as a base to push toward 7,500 or retreats to consolidate.

For energy markets specifically, the WTI move to $83.08 is the key near-term tell. The market had been pricing an Iran de-escalation premium out of crude — WTI fell from the mid-$90s area during the acute tension period toward the upper-$70s to low-$80s as the ceasefire negotiations extended. The overnight reversal suggests the geopolitical premium is repricing back in. A sustained WTI above $84 would begin to pressure consumer spending via gasoline and freight, creating a second-order inflation effect that Powell cannot dismiss as purely supply-shock. Watch whether the $83.08 level holds through the Powell press conference.

Bottom line: The pre-FOMC session is bifurcating into an energy-led / rate-sensitive bid versus a tech/semi headwind. The oil spike is the most acute overnight catalyst; the Powell press conference at 2:30 PM ET is the day's central event. A Powell that frames oil as transitory and confirms the labor market as primary would support the S&P's hold at 7,400+ and likely push ES futures back toward 7,480-7,500. A Powell that sounds more cautious on the inflation path would support the dollar and bonds while pressuring equities — the inverse of Tuesday's pattern, but with energy as the relative outperformer.
