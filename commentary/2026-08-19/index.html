August 19, 2026 | Asia Sells Off (Kospi -5.80%, Nikkei -3.16%) as US Futures Stabilize; FOMC Minutes at 2 PM ET

Asia sold off overnight (Kospi -5.80%, Nikkei -3.16%, Shanghai -2.40%) on Iran-ceasefire overhang and yen strength; US futures stabilize (ES +0.29%) ahead of 2 PM ET FOMC minutes.

The pre-market tape is split: US equity futures are modestly green after Tuesday's -0.69% session, but Asia sold off sharply overnight — the Kospi fell -5.80%, the Nikkei -3.16%, and the Shanghai Composite -2.40% — extending yesterday's risk-off impulse through the yen-strength and exporter-earnings channel. The driver was a continuation of yesterday's US-Iran 60-day ceasefire expiry, with the yen catching a safe-haven bid that compressed Japanese exporter multiples and a softer PBOC liquidity posture that dragged mainland China. Gold extended another +1.28% overnight to $4,421.80 (third consecutive bid session), and the VIX held flat at 15.81 with the term structure still in steep contango at a 9d/30d slope of -14.04% — the canonical "geopolitical premium without panic" regime. The day's open hinges on whether US equities reject or follow Asia's overnight weakness, with the 2:00 PM ET FOMC minutes from the July 28-29 meeting as the structural anchor for the week's narrative.

Overnight Headlines

The structural read: hedged equity exposure with cheap downside insurance remains the structural posture.

The Structural Read

The institutional positioning backdrop has not changed materially since the August 11 COT report (released Friday August 14, now 8 days old): hedge funds are net long S&P 500 E-MINI by +0.53% of open interest, net short Nasdaq-100 Consolidated by -14.19% OI (the largest in the recent sample), net long gold by +54.44% OI, net long USD Index by +43.21% OI, and net long WTI crude by +5.24% OI. The pattern: bullish broad market, bearish AI-infrastructure concentration, hedged with commodities and USD. Today's pre-market tape is consistent — gold's three-session extension and the yen strength that drove Nikkei lower align with the hedge-fund safe-haven bid; the institutional Nasdaq-100 short continues to do work even as NQ futures stabilize. The next COT release (Friday 8/21 at 3:30 PM ET covering Tuesday 8/18 data) will be the first positioning read post-Iran-ceasefire-expiry.

The Asia selloff is the new overnight development. The regional tape was mixed-to-bullish on Monday-Tuesday but turned sharply risk-off overnight as the yen caught a safe-haven bid and the Iran premium compressed exporter earnings through the currency channel. The structural read: the Asia weakness is not a new fundamental driver but a continuation of Tuesday's Iran-ceasefire-overhang applied through a different channel (currency and exporter earnings rather than oil and bonds). US equity futures are rejecting the Asia signal at the index level — ES +0.29%, RTY +0.65% — but NQ remaining modestly negative tells you the AI-cohort positioning trade is still the cleanest signal.

The week's broader setup is unchanged from Tuesday's PM brief: the disinflation pipeline is validated (CPI +0.1% MoM, PPI 0.0% MoM), the growth scare narrative is in flux (Friday's -0.6% MoM Retail Sales was the widest miss in nine months), and the AI capex thesis remains intact. The 7,650–7,850 SPX consolidation range is the technical baseline — Tuesday's close at 7,691.76 is testing the lower bound, and pre-market futures at 7,714.25 are a tentative bounce attempt. A constructive NAHB print or dovish FOMC Minutes preview would defend the range; a hawkish FOMC Minutes combined with continued Asia contagion would extend the downside.

Bottom line: A split tape — US futures stabilize modestly after Tuesday's -0.69% session, but Asia sold off sharply overnight (Kospi -5.80%, Nikkei -3.16%) on continued Iran-premium and yen-strength dynamics. Gold extends a third session to $4,421.80 (+1.28%) as the institutional +54.44% OI long pays off. The day's three macro catalysts (10:00 AM ET NAHB, 2:00 PM ET FOMC Minutes, tomorrow's Initial Jobless Claims) will set the consumer-side narrative ahead of Friday's COT release, next Wednesday's July Core PCE, and Jackson Hole Aug 27-29. The 7,650–7,850 consolidation range is being tested — what changes the read is the NAHB print, the FOMC Minutes' framing of September-cut dispersion, and whether US equities reject or follow Asia's overnight weakness.
