Morning Update — AMZN +12% Pre, AAPL −7%; AI Capex Durability Confirmed by AWS +37%

AMZN +12% premarket after Q2: AWS $42.2B (+37% YoY, fastest since 2021), capex raised to $220B, backlog $496B. AAPL −7% on weak Q3 guide. SPX +1.7% Thu to ~7,441. Chicago PMI 8:45 AM.

Morning Market Analysis — Friday, July 31, 2026

The Q2 earnings tape is sorting AI winners from losers with brutal clarity, and this morning's tape is the proof: Amazon (AMZN) is +12% in premarket trading (~$264) following Thursday's after-close Q2 print that landed the strongest AWS expansion since 2021, while Apple (AAPL) is −7% premarket after issuing weak Q3 guidance on memory-supply constraints. The market is no longer debating whether the AI capex cycle is real — Thursday's MSFT +14.28% and AMZN +12% (premarket) print pair has ended that debate. The debate now is which companies can fund the capex without breaking their free cash flow, and which can monetize it fast enough to justify the spend. S&P 500 futures are tracking the cash close at ~7,441 (Thursday's +1.7% rally recovered all of Wednesday's post-FOMC selloff), VIX at ~18 has fully retraced the post-FOMC spike, and the 10-year yield holds at 4.67%. Today's catalyst stack is thin on macro data but heavy on the AI-winner read-through: Chicago PMI at 8:45 AM ET is the only major print; AMZN's after-hours tape is the structural event.

Overnight Headlines

AMZN +12% Premarket After Q2 Print — AWS +37% YoY, $220B 2026 Capex, $496B Backlog: Thursday after the close, Amazon reported Q2 revenue $200.61B vs $196.47B expected (+20% YoY per LSEG), EPS $5.75 (boosted by $53.4B pre-tax gain from the Anthropic investment mark-up), AWS revenue $42.2B vs $40.54B expected (+37% YoY — fastest growth since 2021), advertising $19.81B vs $19.43B, operating income $27.5B (+43% YoY). Capex guidance raised to $220B for 2026 (from $200B), driven by rising memory costs and demand Amazon says it "will still not have enough capacity to meet." AWS backlog hit $496B — contracted work not yet online. Q3 guidance: revenue $197-202B vs $204.1B consensus (the miss is mechanical, tied to Prime Day being moved from July to June; ex-Prime-Day effect, growth would be +400bps higher per company disclosure). The market's reaction — premarket $264.00 (+12.1% per ts2.tech 04:49 ET quote) — is the cleanest signal yet that AI capex durability is being repriced upward, not downward. The "spend-ahead-of-demand" bear case was killed by AWS's growth print.

AAPL −7% Premarket — Q3 Guide 9-11% Revenue Growth vs 12% Expected on Memory Supply: Apple's earnings, revenue, and iPhone sales all beat consensus, but the Q3 guide of 9-11% revenue growth missed the 12% LSEG consensus on "supply constraints" — specifically a memory shortage that has already pushed Mac and iPad prices higher and is expected to push iPhone prices up this year. Apple's lack of a massive capex expansion was supposed to make it the defensive AI trade — but a memory-supply-driven revenue ceiling means it can't grow into the AI-driven device replacement cycle either. Premarket −7% reflects the realization that Apple's "cheap relative to AI-spenders" thesis has a supply-side ceiling. Note: AAPL is now +23% YTD vs AMZN +4% YTD per CNBC; the relative rotation is the trade.

MSFT +14.28% Thursday — Monster Day Validates AI-Spend-to-Profit Translation: Microsoft rallied 14.28% on Thursday — its biggest single-session gain — as investors concluded that the company's AI capex is now translating into reported profit growth. Combined with META −8% Thursday on capex concerns, the market is drawing a sharp line: companies whose AI spend is showing revenue/efficiency returns trade violently higher; companies whose spend looks open-ended trade violently lower. AMZN's overnight confirmation extends this trade into Friday's session. META's selloff Thursday creates a head-to-head framing that may extend into today's tape.

June PCE Confirms Disinflation: Core +0.1% MoM, +3.3% YoY (Released 7/30): The Fed's preferred inflation gauge landed at core PCE +0.1% MoM, +3.3% YoY for June 2026, down from May's +3.4% YoY per Advisor Perspectives. Headline PCE printed +0.1% MoM; personal spending rose +0.3%. Warsh said the cooler June data did not factor significantly into Wednesday's hold decision — the FOMC's 9-3 hold was about labor and financial conditions, not the latest inflation print. The next PCE release covering July is August 26. Today's tape implication: the disinflation trend is intact but slow, which supports the bull case for rate-sensitive cyclicals but limits the urgency of an early September cut.

What to Watch at the Open

  1. AMZN +12% vs AAPL −7% — is the AI-winner rotation extending or fading at the bell? The premarket divergence is the cleanest expression of the new tape thesis: companies whose AI capex is generating measurable revenue growth are being rewarded; companies whose capex is open-ended or whose growth is supply-constrained are being penalized. AMZN's $42.2B AWS print (+37% YoY, fastest since 2021) is the gold-standard confirmation; AAPL's Q3 9-11% guide on memory supply is the gold-standard denial. If AMZN holds above $260 into the cash open, the AI-capex-durable trade extends to GOOGL, NVDA, and the broader chip complex. If AMZN fades back below $250 by 10:00 AM ET, the rotation loses momentum and the tape reverts to mean-reversion. Watch NVDA pre-market tape as the read-through.
  1. SPX at ~7,441 — does the post-FOMC + post-AMZN rally push toward the 7,500 path? Thursday's +1.7% SPX close recovered all of Wednesday's −1.52% post-FOMC selloff, and AMZN's overnight +12% adds another ~+50bp to the index open. A clean break above 7,460 in the first 30 minutes re-activates the 7,500 target path from the Wednesday afternoon forecast; consolidation in the 7,420-7,460 range reflects the VIX reversion pricing in the rally; a fade back below 7,420 keeps the post-FOMC range-trade intact.
  1. VIX at ~18 vs SPX +1.7% — is the vol regime normalizing or compressing too far? VIX reversion from 20.66 (Wed) to ~18 (Thu) alongside the SPX +1.7% rally is the textbook "risk-on re-anchor." A VIX print below 17 by 10:00 AM ET today would signal that protective put demand is being unwound aggressively — historically a leading indicator of melt-up conditions but also of complacency into the August tape (no Fed meetings, no major data, summer doldrums). A VIX hold at 18-19 suggests the market still respects the post-FOMC range and is hedging into the weekend. Friday's close is the last tape-setting event before the Monday AMZN earnings follow-through.

The Structural Read

The interaction between the AMZN +12% premarket move and the AAPL −7% premarket move is the cleanest expression of the current tape regime: the market is sorting AI-related businesses into three buckets — (1) AI-spend-to-profit translators (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL — rewarded with violent upside on confirmation); (2) AI-spend-as-headwind (META — punished on capex concerns); (3) AI-adjacent but supply-constrained (AAPL — punished on guidance despite beating on reported numbers). The structural bull case is that bucket (1) is broadening; the structural bear case is that bucket (1) is narrow and concentrated in 3-4 names, leaving the index exposed if any of them disappoints in 2H 2026. The 10Y at 4.67% suggests the bond market is comfortable with both readings — not pricing recession, not pricing reacceleration.

The post-FOMC reversion (VIX 20.66 → 18, SPX 7,316 → 7,441) is complete in a single session, which is a structural signal in itself: the Warsh Fed's 9-3 hawkish-dissent hold was fully absorbed by Thursday's close, and the AMZN-led AI-capex re-rating is now the dominant tape driver through Friday and into Monday's open. The path of least resistance for the next two weeks is consolidation in the 7,400-7,500 range as the market digests AMZN's $220B capex raise and waits for the August 26 PCE release for the next inflation read. For vol positioning, VIX at 18 with SPX at 7,441 is a regime that supports premium-selling over premium-buying; for trend-following, the AI-capex-durable basket (NVDA, AVGO, AMZN, MSFT) is the leadership cluster.

Bottom line: AMZN's overnight +12% is the structural event of the week — AWS growth re-acceleration to +37% YoY validates the AI-capex-durable thesis that Wednesday's hawkish-dissent FOMC briefly threatened. Friday's tape is a confirmation test: if AMZN holds above $260 into the close and SPX holds above 7,440, the AI-capex re-rating extends into August. If AMZN fades below $250 or SPX gives back the AMZN-driven gap-up, the rally is a single-session event and the post-FOMC range-trade reasserts. Chicago PMI at 8:45 AM ET is the only macro print; the AI tape is the dominant signal. Watch NVDA and the chip complex as the read-through — they were Thursday's casualty and Friday's most leveraged re-rating candidate.
