August 18, 2026 | NQ Drops 1.1% Overnight as Tech Leads the Pre-Market Tape

NQ -1.13% leads ES -0.45% as tech extends weakness; VIX +3.75% to 15.76, term slope -21.46%. July Housing 8:30 ET (1.35M consensus); FOMC Minutes tomorrow.

The pre-market tape is shaped by a single dominant signal: Nasdaq-100 futures are down -1.13% to 29,755, dragging S&P 500 futures (ES) to 7,733.75 (-0.45%) and Russell 2000 (RTY) to 3,056.10 (-0.29%). The Dow (YM) is flat at 53,537. The tech-led gap mirrors the institutional positioning backdrop — hedge funds' CFTC COT report shows a -14.19% OI net short in Nasdaq-100, the largest in the recent sample. The defensive bid is back: gold futures (GC=F) added +0.84% to $4,454.80, extending last week's haven theme. VIX ticked up to 15.76 (+3.75% from Monday's 15.19 close) but the 9d/30d term slope stayed in steep contango at -21.46%, keeping the cost of protection cheap. Today's calendar centers on the 8:30 AM ET July Housing Starts & Building Permits release (consensus 1.35M starts, Action Economics; prior June 1.329M), followed by Industrial Production at 9:15 AM ET and Pending Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET. Tomorrow's FOMC Minutes (2:00 PM ET) is the structural anchor for the week.

Overnight Headlines

Nasdaq-100 Futures Lead Pre-Market Lower (-1.13% to 29,755) — Tech Cohort Extends Yesterday's Underperformance: Nasdaq-100 futures fell -1.13% overnight to 29,755, the dominant pre-market signal heading into Tuesday's session. The move extends Monday's underperformance in mega-cap tech — Communication Services (XLC) closed at 112.55 (-1.89%, the day's worst sector). Per the August 11 CFTC COT report (released Friday August 14 at 3:30 PM ET, 7 days old as of today), hedge funds hold a net short in Nasdaq-100 Consolidated at -14.19% of open interest (-42,905 contracts) — the largest such position in the recent sample. The smart-money positioning is bearish Nasdaq-100, and the pre-market tape is following that lead.

Gold Extends Bid to $4,454.80 (+0.84%) — Haven Demand Reasserts as Risk Assets Soften: Gold futures (GC=F) added +0.84% to $4,454.80 overnight, extending Monday's +1.00% gain and the prior week's structural bid pattern. The move aligns with hedge funds' aggressive +54.44% OI net long in gold (per the August 11 CFTC COT report) — the highest end of the multi-year range. The driver is the structural mix that's been in place since mid-2024: soft-landing probability, USD softness (DXY at 99.643), and an accumulating safe-haven premium. The convergence of tech-led equity weakness, modest VIX bid (+3.75%), and gold strength is the canonical "late-cycle haven rotation" tape. Oil did not follow gold's lead — WTI crude (CL=F) is -0.62% to $83.98 — signaling the move is risk-premium, not a growth-scare.

Asia Mixed: Hang Seng +1.41% and Shanghai +1.61% Lead; Nikkei -1.83% on Yen Strength: Asia equities were mixed overnight. The Hang Seng closed +1.41% at 25,471.15 and the Shanghai Composite gained +1.61% to 3,990.30, continuing the late-July pattern of China-led outperformance on PBOC liquidity support. The Nikkei 225 fell -1.83% to 67,460.73 as the yen strengthened against the dollar — a continuation of the BoJ normalization trade that compresses exporter earnings. European indices opened slightly weaker: DAX at 26,251.67 (-0.71% from Friday) and FTSE at 10,722.19 (-0.26%). The takeaway: the overnight Asia/Europe session confirmed the direction — modest, not bearish — with the China bid offsetting the Japan selloff.

The Structural Read

The institutional positioning backdrop entering this week has not changed materially since the August 11 COT report (released Friday August 14, 7 days old as of today): hedge funds are net long S&P 500 E-MINI by +0.53% of OI, net short Nasdaq-100 Consolidated by -14.19% OI (the largest in the recent sample), net long gold by +54.44% OI, net long USD Index by +43.21% OI, and net long WTI crude by +5.24% OI. The pattern is consistent: bullish broad market, bearish AI-infrastructure concentration, hedged with commodities and USD. The pre-market tape is consistent with that positioning — NQ leads down, ES follows modestly, gold extends, oil does not. Smart money is not bailing on the broad market; it's leaning into the AI-cohort skepticism that the AMAT post-earnings reaction exposed last week.

Tomorrow's FOMC Minutes is the structural anchor for the week. The committee held rates at 5.25–5.50% on July 28–29 but began signaling a September cut path. The minutes will reveal committee dispersion on three questions: (1) is September cut the base case or still debated, (2) SEP dot-plot revision framing, (3) the labor market's read — "robust" or "cooling." A hawkish-leaning minutes would extend the 10Y's repricing toward 4.75% (the level that challenges the multiple-expansion thesis); a dovish-leaning minutes would compress 10Y yields and likely trigger institutional Nasdaq-100 short-covering.

The week's broader setup is unchanged: the disinflation pipeline has been validated (CPI +0.1% MoM, PPI 0.0% MoM), the growth scare narrative is in flux (Friday's -0.6% MoM Retail Sales was the widest miss in nine months), and the AI capex thesis remains intact. The 7,750–7,850 SPX consolidation range is the technical baseline — pre-market futures at 7,733.75 are testing the lower bound. A constructive housing print at 8:30 AM ET or a dovish FOMC Minutes preview would defend the range; a miss combined with continued tech weakness would extend the downside.

Bottom line: A tech-led pre-market with NQ -1.13% as the dominant signal. Gold and modest VIX bid complete the haven-rotation tape. The day's three macro prints will set the consumer-side narrative ahead of tomorrow's FOMC Minutes. The institutional positioning backdrop is intact: bullish broad market, bearish Nasdaq-100, hedged with commodities. The 7,750–7,850 consolidation range is being tested — what changes the read is the housing print and tomorrow's minutes.
