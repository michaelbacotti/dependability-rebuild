Warsh's Hawkish Jackson Hole Verdict Drives Friday's Tape; Oil Snaps Back, Equities Hold

Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole verdict backed 10Y to 4.72%, crushed gold -3.24%. Equities absorbed without panic. Oil snapped to $86+. ISM, JOLTS, NFP this week.

Friday's session absorbed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote as a verdict-absorption day rather than a panic day: the S&P 500 closed -0.25% (7,711.76 from Thursday's 7,730.99, per Cboe SPX) — the intraday low was only -0.50%, recovering before the close. The 10-year Treasury yield backed up +5 basis points on the day to 4.72% (per FRED DGS10), gold sold off hard to $408.89 (-3.24% from $422.60 Thursday, per SPDR GLD), and WTI crude reversed course from its post-ceasefire $83.40 close, snapping back to approximately $86 area by Friday's settlement as geopolitical risk premium reasserted itself. The structural read: the equity market absorbed Warsh's hawkish/less-dovish Jackson Hole framing — which emphasized the Fed's 2% inflation commitment and AI-disinflation skepticism — without breaking, consistent with the institutional framework having priced hawkish-Warsh as the higher-probability scenario heading into the keynote. Monday August 31 is the first trading session after the Warsh verdict; the calendar is light, with the next major macro print being Tuesday September 2's August ISM Manufacturing PMI.

Overnight Headlines

Oil reverses course — WTI snaps back to approximately $86 area by Friday settlement from the post-ceasefire $83.40 close, +geopolitical risk premium reasserts. The post-ceasefire oil-fade thesis (which had driven WTI from ~$94 area in mid-August toward the mid-$80s) paused and partially reversed on Friday. The ceasefire regime between Israel and Hamas, which had allowed oil to consolidate the geopolitical risk premium over the preceding two weeks, produced a floor under WTI near $83-84 area. The snap-back to ~$86 on Friday reflects renewed geopolitical uncertainty — ceasefire durability questions, potential supply disruption scenarios — re-emerging as a market factor heading into the week of August 31. VIX compressed further to 14.43 Friday (-0.55% from Thursday's 14.51; -6.60% week-to-date) — the cheapest print since mid-July, extending the compressed-floor tape despite hawkish-Warsh. The institutional options framework absorbed the Warsh verdict with further vol compression rather than expansion — the canonical "event treated as confirming rather than regime-changing" signal. The term structure continues in contango: the 9-day implied vol (^VIX9D) is estimated near 13.09, versus the 14.43 spot VIX, suggesting the near-term protection is cheaper than the 30-day. VVIX (vol of vol) is estimated near 82-86 area. One framework at VIX 14.43: front-month implied vol sits near its floor relative to the past six weeks, leaving limited room for further compression unless the September calendar confirms the no-cut-consensus thesis without incident.

  • Tue Sep 2, 10:00 AM ET — August ISM Manufacturing PMI (consensus ~49.5 per FactSet, prior 49.0 in July): The consensus reading just below 50 is consistent with the post-ceasefire industrial cycle still running below the expansion threshold. Per Institute for Supply Management, any reading above 50 signals manufacturing expansion; below 50 signals contraction. The July print of 49.0 was already in contraction territory. Watch for any acceleration or deceleration in the new orders and employment sub-indices — those are the leading components that most directly inform the August jobs report framing ahead of the Sep 4 NFP.
  • Wed Sep 3, 10:00 AM ET — July JOLTS Job Openings (consensus ~7.20M openings, prior 7.21M in June): The labor market churn measure. Per BLS JOLTS, 7.21M June openings placed job openings near the lower end of the 2024-2026 range (high was ~12M in 2022). A print near 7.20M would be consistent with a gradually cooling but not collapsing labor market. The quits rate and hires rate are the sub-indices that most closely track wage inflation dynamics. The next JOLTS release will be among the inputs feeding the Sep 4 NFP framing.
  • Thu Sep 4, 8:30 AM ET — August NFP (consensus +75K nonfarm, prior +114K in July; unemployment consensus ~4.3%, prior 4.3%): The structural labor market verdict for September. Per BLS NFP, +114K July NFP was below the +185K consensus — a soft print that the no-cut-consensus regime had absorbed without breaking. The August consensus of +75K reflects expectations of further cooling. A print above +100K would be consistent with the labor market remaining resilient; below +75K would deepen the softening trend. This is the primary macro input ahead of the September 16 FOMC.
