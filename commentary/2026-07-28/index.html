Overnight Headlines

The "Korea Chip Shock" is reigniting AI-memory selling — CXMT IPO surges 530%, ASML -7% Monday, $1T+ wiped from chip complex. The most important overnight development is the China/Korea memory story. ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) priced its STAR Market IPO at 8.66 yuan and traded to ~54.65 yuan on debut — a +530% surge that raised 57.9 billion yuan (~$8.5 billion) and pushed CXMT's market cap to ~3.66 trillion yuan (~$540 billion). The Information reported Monday that a Chinese state-backed firm has begun producing domestically-developed 193nm immersion DUV systems — five units this year, ~20 in 2027, well below ASML's 131 immersion DUV systems shipped last year but a structural signal of China's chipmaking self-sufficiency push. ASML fell -7%+ Monday; the Direxion Daily South Korea Bull 3X (KORU) is down -15% overnight. More than $1 trillion was wiped from U.S. stocks Monday, with more than half the losses concentrated in six memory-related chip stocks. For long-term investors: China is no longer a marginal player in mature-node DUV and DRAM, and Western AI-memory names will need to compete on yield, reliability, and HBM technology (where ASML's EUV monopoly and Samsung/SK Hynix's HBM leadership still hold a 2-3 year structural advantage).

NVDA down -1.11% pre-market at $194.32 despite the $5B SSI announcement — an Nvidia employee detained in Taiwan on alleged AI-chip smuggling. Two NVDA stories layered Monday/Tuesday. First, Bloomberg reported Nvidia has committed $5 billion to Safe Superintelligence (SSI), the AI startup founded by OpenAI co-founder Ilya Sutskever; SSI will also gain access to Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin platform. NVDA fell as much as -5.3% Monday midday on the broader chip selloff despite the SSI news. Second, Taiwanese prosecutors detained a Nvidia employee suspected of falsifying documents to smuggle advanced AI chips to China — reinforcing the export-control regime around Nvidia's leading-edge silicon. Pre-market action: NVDA $194.32 (-1.11%), XLK $171.66 (-1.51% from Monday close $174.30), QQQ $674.99 (-1.04% from Monday close $682.12). The structural read: the AI-capex cycle is intact, but the timing of FCF conversion at the hyperscaler layer (Alphabet $195-205B 2026 capex raise last week; MSFT/META Wednesday; AAPL/AMZN Thursday) is the market's next test.

FOMC Day 1 begins today; Wednesday's rate decision + Powell press conference is the marquee event. The Federal Open Market Committee convenes its two-day meeting Tuesday-Wednesday, with the rate decision Wednesday July 29 at 2:00 PM ET and Powell press conference at 2:30 PM ET. Consensus expectation is a hold at the current 3.50-3.75% range (no dot plot update — the next Summary of Economic Projections is September 15-16). Powell's framing language around the September cut path is the market-moving lever; current markets pricing implies roughly 60% probability of a September rate reduction. The data backdrop is mixed but on balance soft: Atlanta Fed GDPNow is now tracking Q2 GDP at 1.7% (down sharply from ~3% earlier this quarter — a notable downgrade), Monday's June Durable Goods Orders came in at +0.3% MoM (below +1.6% consensus but with +0.6% ex-transportation confirming underlying business capex is steady), and the VIX held at 18.67 Monday. The 10-year Treasury yield at 4.64% (down 4 bps Monday from Friday's 4.68%, with TLT holding at $83.93 pre-market) keeps the September cut trade alive.

Magnificent Seven earnings blitz begins Wednesday with MSFT and META — followed by AAPL and AMZN Thursday. Microsoft (MSFT) and Meta (META) report Wednesday after-close — consensus META $7.20 EPS on $60.21B revenue. The watch-item: AI capex guidance for FY2027 — Alphabet raised 2026 capex to $195-205B last week (from $180-190B), and the market is parsing whether MSFT and META will follow with similarly aggressive top-ups that confirm the multi-year cycle, or signal capex moderation that validates the FCF-compression narrative. Apple (AAPL) and Amazon (AMZN) report Thursday after-close — consensus AMZN ~$196B revenue, $1.81-1.83 EPS, with AWS growth (~+18% YoY) the read-through for the cloud thesis. AMKR also reports after-close today.

The Structural Read

The Korea chip shock is a supply-chain diversification event, not a thesis break — and the AI-memory rout is creating the entry point the Q3 bull case needed. Three things are happening simultaneously in the chip complex, and the market is conflating them. (1) CXMT's blockbuster IPO confirms China is now a structural player in mature-node DRAM — the 530% debut, $540B market cap, and strategic-investor syndicate (Xiaomi, Chery, Alibaba, ZTE) confirm the China memory complex has institutional credibility. (2) China's domestic DUV progress (5 immersion DUV systems in 2026 vs ASML's 131 last year) is real but structurally constrained — Chinese chipmakers will need months to validate accuracy, reliability, and compatibility before production deployment. ASML's EUV monopoly remains intact. (3) The $1T+ Monday wipeout was concentrated in six memory-related names — a liquidity- and sentiment-driven event, not a fundamental-demand break. For long-term positioning: the AI-memory complex (MU, SNDK) is now pricing in a structural margin reset; the entry-point question for Q3 is whether Wednesday's MSFT/META prints confirm capex durability.

The cross-asset setup into FOMC Wednesday is structurally constructive — and the tape is positioning for a measured, data-dependent hold. Three developments since last week's inflation scare are meaningful: (1) oil has held near $84 (USO $123.71, -9.5% from Friday close), removing the acute inflation-premium that drove the 10Y to 4.70% Thursday; (2) the 10Y has retreated to 4.64% Monday from Thursday's 4.70% spike — a 6 bp pullback that keeps the September-cut trade alive at ~60% probability; (3) Atlanta Fed GDPNow at 1.7% is the new dovish data point that gives Powell political cover to acknowledge Q3 growth moderation without confirming recession. The VIX at 18.67 Monday (+0.48% on day, +9.5% over five trading days) is still well below the 19-20 zone that would signal acute de-risking. The smart positioning: stay allocated into Wednesday's FOMC + earnings, use any chip-rout extension as a tactical entry window for MU/SNDK/AMKR (CoWoS exposure), and watch AMKR's after-close reaction as the pre-MSFT/META leading indicator.

The structural uptrend is intact; the week is a four-layer event. AMKR tonight, ADP + Case-Shiller + 2Y auction today, FOMC + MSFT/META Wednesday, PCE + Q2 GDP + AAPL/AMZN Friday. AI infrastructure has 2-3 years of forward visibility (TSMC $60-64B 2026 capex, ASML's EUV dominance); the hyperscaler application layer will have a 12-18 month FCF-conversion timeline under pressure until the capex cycle matures. The chip rout creates entry-point opportunities in the memory and packaging complex (MU, SNDK, AMKR, ASML on weakness); the broader equity market is holding near all-time-high territory with the 10Y at 4.64% and oil near $84 — both constructive for the September-cut probability. The risk-reward into Wednesday's FOMC + earnings cluster is asymmetric to the upside if MSFT/META capex guidance lands in the $90-100B FY2027 range with bullish AI-revenue commentary.

Bottom line: The tape enters FOMC Day 1 in a two-track pattern — chip-led tech weakness (NVDA -1.11%, XLK -1.51%, QQQ -1.04% pre-market on the Korea/China memory shock) offsetting a defensive-bid broader market (XLV +1.28%, XLF +0.53%, IWM -0.11%, oil near $84, 10Y at 4.64%). Wednesday's FOMC + MSFT/META is the binary confirmation of the AI-capex structural thesis. The September-cut probability at ~60% is the single most important rate-path variable for Q3 positioning.
