Sector themes cycle in and out of focus, but the structural themes — the buildout of AI infrastructure, the commercialization of low-Earth orbit, the US energy production complex, and the role of hard assets in a structurally higher-rate world — don't. They're all capital-intensive, multi-decade trends with their own supply-demand dynamics and their own sentiment regimes. This pillar covers the four we're tracking, with the option-volatility signature and the positioning implications for each.

How to use this page. Each sub-hub covers one theme. The articles inside each hub focus on the structural thesis, the key data points to track, and where options positioning makes sense (and where it doesn't). The themes compound — AI infrastructure drives the power-grid buildout, which drives the next energy story.

Sub-Hubs

AI Infrastructure: The Hyperscaler Capex Supercycle

The structural story behind the AI trade: hyperscaler capex commitments ($300B+ annually across Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta), the capex-to-revenue ratio, the build-versus-licensing economics, the power-grid bottleneck, and the implications for semis, networking, and the utilities complex. The AI trade is a 5–10 year capex cycle — not a 12-month narrative.

Why it matters for options: AI infrastructure names have unusually rich implied vol (40–60% IV on the leaders). Calendar spreads, iron condors, and covered calls all behave differently when single-name earnings can move a stock 15% in a session.

Space Economy: From Constellation to Industrialization

The space economy has shifted from a government-funded exploration story to a private-market industrial buildout. SpaceX, Cerebras, the satellite-internet buildouts (Starlink, Kuiper), launch cadence, and the implications for existing defense and aerospace primes. The capital-formation story (blockbuster IPOs) matters as much as the operating story.

Why it matters for options: Pure-play space exposure is mostly through private companies (SpaceX) or ETFs (UFO, ARKX). Public exposure through established primes has a different volatility signature — slower-moving, more defense-budget driven.

Read the Space Economy hub →

Energy: The Oil Paradox and US Dominance

The structural story behind US energy dominance: shale production economics, OPEC+ discipline, the role of US LNG exports in global pricing, the refining bottleneck, and the paradox of being both the world's largest producer and a structurally long-term demand buyer. Energy isn't a 2020s narrative — it's the 2020s through 2030s cycle.

Why it matters for options: Energy names have a fundamentally different vol regime than tech — earnings are driven by commodity prices, not multiples. Calendar spreads and ratio spreads outperform directional plays in this regime.

Read the Energy hub →

Precious Metals: Gold as a Store of Value in a New Macro Regime

Gold and silver in a structurally higher-rate, structurally higher-deficit regime. The 50-year data, the central-bank reserve dynamic, the role of gold in a multi-asset portfolio when bond-equity correlation has flipped, and whether the precious-metals trade is now a monetary regime trade rather than an inflation trade.

Why it matters for options: GLD has low vol (~15% IV) and high contango, making it ideal for covered-call income strategies. SLV is more volatile and rewards calendar-spread positioning. Mining equities (GDX) have option vol regimes closer to tech than to commodities.
