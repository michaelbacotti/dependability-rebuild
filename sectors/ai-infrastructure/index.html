AI Infrastructure: The Hyperscaler Capex Supercycle

AI infrastructure is a 5–10 year capex cycle, not a 12-month narrative.

Advertisement

AI infrastructure is a 5–10 year capex cycle, not a 12-month narrative. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta collectively committed over $300B in capex in 2025 — and 2026 guidance is higher. The investment thesis isn't about the AI model layer (where competition is fierce) — it's about the infrastructure layer (where the four hyperscalers have pricing power). This sub-hub covers the structural story, the data to track, and the option-volatility signature of the trade.

The Hyperscaler Capex Math

Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta committed $312B in capex for 2025 (collectively). 2026 guidance implies $340–380B. The capex-to-revenue ratio for the four is now 23% — well above the 12–15% historical norm. The thesis is that AI compute demand exceeds supply, and that owning the infrastructure captures the bulk of the value. The bear case is that revenue lags capex by 18–24 months and that the eventual monetization is uncertain.

The cleanest way to track the AI infrastructure story: capex commitments, data-center power-purchase agreement (PPA) announcements, and AI-attributed revenue (Azure AI, Google Cloud AI, AWS Bedrock, etc.). When AI-attributed revenue growth exceeds 80%, the capex cycle is justified. When it falls below 50%, the cycle is in question.

The Power-Grid Bottleneck

Data centers require enormous amounts of power. New data centers in Virginia, Ohio, and Texas are constrained by interconnection queues — typically 3–7 years from announcement to commissioning. The AI buildout is therefore limited not by compute supply (chips, networking) but by power supply (utility interconnection, transmission, generation). This bottleneck is structurally favorable for utilities (XLU) and renewables (TAN, ICLN) but is a headwind for the timeline of AI-revenue growth.

Two trades follow. (1) Long utilities + long AI compute names as a paired position captures the bottleneck economics. (2) Long volatility on data-center REITs (DLR, EQIX) ahead of earnings where the power-cost commentary is most likely to surface.

Build vs. License: The Economics

Hyperscalers have a choice: build their own AI models (high capex, full control, full risk) or license third-party models (low capex, low control, low risk). The current strategy is to do both — build frontier models for capability, license third-party models for breadth. The economics: building a frontier model costs $300M–$1B per training run; licensing third-party API calls costs pennies per query. The build path is justified only if the resulting capability drives 30%+ better product pricing.

Implication: AI infrastructure names are exposed to both capex-cycle economics (the build side) and the application-layer economics (the revenue side). Earnings calls increasingly disclose both — and the spread between them is the trade.

Options Positioning on AI Names

AI infrastructure names (MSFT, GOOG, AMZN, META) have implied vol of 35–50% — well above broader market vol (12–18%). The high vol is justified by earnings dispersion around capex/revenue dynamics. Calendar spreads and iron condors work exceptionally well in this regime: earnings is the dominant vol event, and the 2-week pre-earnings IV ramp creates a tradable vol premium. Avoid naked short volatility in the final week before earnings — gamma risk is too high.

Specific setups: (1) Sell calendar spreads 30–45 DTE, capturing the IV term-structure steepness. (2) Sell iron condors 14–21 DTE post-earnings, capturing vol crush. (3) Buy long-dated calls (LEAPs) on names that have lagged the capex guidance — the catch-up trades can run for 6+ months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the AI buildout really a multi-decade cycle or a 2024–2025 bubble?

The capex commitments are too large and too slow-moving to be a single-year narrative. The four hyperscalers have committed $650B+ in cumulative capex through 2027, with multi-year PPA contracts and physical data-center construction timelines of 18–24 months. Whether it generates commensurate revenue over 5–10 years is the open question — but the capex itself is structural, not narrative-driven.

What data should I watch to track AI infrastructure health?

Three numbers matter: (1) Hyperscaler quarterly capex (consensus expects it to keep rising through 2026). (2) AI-attributed revenue at each hyperscaler (Azure AI, Google Cloud AI, AWS Bedrock, Meta AI). (3) Data-center utilization rates (most hyperscalers don't disclose this, but earnings commentary hints at it). When AI revenue growth exceeds 80%, the capex cycle is justified. Below 50%, the cycle is in trouble.

What's the right option strategy for AI infrastructure names?

Calendar spreads (sell front-month, buy back-month at the same strike) and iron condors (sell OTM put spread + OTM call spread) are the workhorse strategies. Vol is high enough that the premiums compensate for the directional risk. Avoid long single-leg positions unless you have explicit earnings-day conviction.
