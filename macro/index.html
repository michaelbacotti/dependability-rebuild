Macro & Rates

Inflation, the Federal Reserve, the long-Treasury regime, and the dollar — and how each translates into options positioning. Macro is the regime in which every strategy on Dependability.us either works or fails.

Advertisement

Macro isn't decoration. It determines whether an iron condor collects premium or gets run through both strikes, whether a long volatility position pays for its theta decay, and whether the 60/40 portfolio still functions as advertised. The 2026 macro regime is structurally different from the pre-pandemic regime — Kevin Warsh's New Era Fed, persistent fiscal deficits pushing long-end yields higher, and a dollar that can rally on relative-rate advantage rather than growth. This pillar is the macro floor under every other piece of research on the site.

How to use this page. Each sub-hub covers one macro driver — the Fed's policy path, the bond regime, the inflation regime, the dollar's role. The articles inside each hub are written for traders who already understand options, so the focus is on what the regime implies for positioning, not on introductory macro.

Sub-Hubs

Inflation: The Measurement Problem

What CPI and PCE actually measure (and what they don't), how shelter and owners' equivalent rent distort the prints, why Trimmed Mean PCE has become the Fed's preferred gauge, and why the published inflation numbers lag the underlying trend by 6–12 months. The post-pandemic inflation regime is fundamentally about measurement choices as much as it is about money supply.

Why it matters for options: CPI prints move the dollar, the curve, and implied volatility on the same morning. Reading the print correctly — and not trusting the headline number — is a structural edge.

Read the Inflation hub →

Fed Policy: Kevin Warsh's New Era

The regime change at the Federal Reserve. From forward guidance and reaction-function predictability to data-dependence and structural skepticism of the Phillips Curve. Kevin Warsh's first moves, the implications for the dot plot, the end of MMT-era accommodation, and the rate-cycle positioning rules for options traders. This is the macro driver that determines whether long-dated options rally or sell off.

Why it matters for options: Rho (the option's sensitivity to rates) on a 12-month LEAP is non-trivial. The end of the zero-rate era changed the discount-rate term in every Black-Scholes calculation. Long calls became more expensive on rate moves alone.

Read the Fed Policy hub →

Rates & Bonds: The New Regime

Why long Treasury yields now set the risk-asset backdrop instead of following it. The fiscal-deficit overhang, the term premium re-emergence, the death of the 60/40's negative correlation, and the implications for TLT, equities, and growth-stock multiples. The bond regime changed structurally in 2022 and is not reverting to the pre-pandemic era.

Why it matters for options: Bond-equity correlation flipped. That changes every multi-asset hedge, every collar, every covered-call-plus-bonds income sleeve. The macro input to equity-option pricing now comes through rates more than through earnings revisions.

Read the Rates & Bonds hub →

FX & Dollar: The Dollar's Lever

The dollar's strength and weakness as a structural lever on global risk-asset pricing. The DXY mechanics, the relationship between real yields and the dollar, the carry-trade unwind risk, and the asymmetric impact of dollar strength on US multinationals versus emerging-market debt. The 2026 dollar regime is not a mirror of the 2001–2020 era.

Why it matters for options: DXY moves drive cross-asset volatility. A 5% dollar move in two weeks is a vol event, not a direction event. It forces deltas across the entire options book to be re-priced at higher implied vol.
