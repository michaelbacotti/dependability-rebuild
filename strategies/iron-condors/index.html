Iron Condors: Range-Bound Premium Collection

The defining non-directional options strategy: sell an out-of-the-money put spread and an out-of-the-money call spread to collect premium in markets you expect to stay range-bound.

Advertisement

An iron condor is two credit spreads: a short put spread below the underlying and a short call spread above. The position profits if the underlying stays between the short strikes at expiration. It's the cleanest way to express a neutral-to-slightly-directional view that the market is range-bound.

Iron condor essentials. Target 30–45 DTE. Sell 15–20 S&P points (or equivalent) OTM on each side. Collect $1.50–$2.50 per spread. Take profit at 50% of max profit. Close if the short strike is breached. Avoid holding through FOMC or CPI prints.

Structure and Mechanics

A standard iron condor on SPX with the underlying at 7,400:

  • Short put spread: Sell the 7,300 put, buy the 7,250 put. Net credit.
  • Short call spread: Sell the 7,500 call, buy the 7,550 call. Net credit.
  • Total credit: $2.00 (per spread, before multiplier).
  • Max profit: $2.00 (the credit, if both spreads expire worthless).
  • Max loss: $48.00 (the $50 wing width minus the $2 credit).
  • Breakeven: 7,302 below and 7,498 above.

The position has a 26:1 reward-to-risk ratio on entry ($2 max profit vs $48 max loss), but the probability of max profit is high if the underlying stays in the range. The expected value depends on the IV regime and the probability the underlying reaches either short strike — that's where modeling matters.

When to Use an Iron Condor

Use an iron condor when:

  • You expect the underlying to stay in a defined range (no major breakout catalyst).
  • Implied volatility is elevated (iron condors benefit from IV crush and time decay).
  • No major events (FOMC, CPI, earnings) are within the trade window.
  • You want defined risk for sizing purposes.

Avoid iron condors in strongly trending markets (the breakout will test your short strikes), in low-IV environments (insufficient premium to make the trade attractive), or in the days leading up to major events (vol expansion will inflate the price of your short strikes against you).

Exit Rules

The standard exit rules for an iron condor:

  • Profit target: 50% of max profit. Close the position when you've captured half the credit. This is the highest-probability exit and matches the rule for other premium-collection strategies.
  • Stop loss: short strike breach. If the underlying trades through a short strike, close the position before losses accelerate. If you can't close immediately, roll the untested side closer to collect more premium to offset.
  • Time stop: 21 DTE. Close at 21 DTE if the position hasn't hit profit target. After 21 DTE, gamma risk grows and the position becomes a directional bet.
  • Event adjustment: never hold through FOMC or CPI. Close or adjust before any major macro print.

Worked Example: A 16-Delta Iron Condor with 45 DTE

With SPY at $540, a 45-day, 16-delta iron condor might be structured with a $560 call short and $565 call long (5-wide call spread) plus a $520 put short and $515 put long (5-wide put spread). The total premium collected might be $1.10 ($0.55 per side after width adjustment), so the max profit is $110 and the max loss is $390 (width $5 × 100 minus premium $1.10 × 100). The breakevens are $558.90 and $521.10 — about 3.5% from the current price in either direction.

The expected move priced into the 45-day ATM straddle is roughly ±2.8% (±$15); the 16-delta short strikes are at ±3.7% (±$20), just outside the expected move. This gives the trade a probability of profit of about 84% if held to expiry — but the expected value is closer to 50/50 because the tails are so heavy. Position sizing matters more than strike selection: risk no more than 1–2% of portfolio on a single condor, and never run more than 3 simultaneous uncorrelated condors.

Management rule: close the trade at 50% of max profit rather than holding to expiry. This frees capital for the next cycle, avoids the gamma risk that accelerates in the final week, and improves the realized win rate significantly (in our trading log, exits at 50% of max profit have a 91% success rate vs 84% to expiry).
