What Drove the Tape

The structural read is unambiguous: mega-cap AI capex durability has now been validated at three of the three highest-profile bars of Q2 (TSMC capex raise earlier, MSFT +15% post-earnings surge Wed, AMZN AWS +37% YoY beat Thu after-close). The market is interpreting the AMZN print as the second definitive validation of the Warsh-Fed-pause-with-hawkish-dissent thesis: the Fed paused with hawkish dissents, MSFT printed, AMZN printed, the AI capex multiplier held, and equity multiples expanded. For positioning: this is the cleanest possible outcome for the post-FOMC re-rate. The AMZN print extends the MSFT validation into Friday's close, and the path to 7,500 is now a 1-day tape away.

The second story is June PCE's first negative-MoM print since mid-2022 — a major disinflation signal. The BEA released the June Personal Income and Outlays report this morning at 8:30 AM ET (BEA, CNBC, PNC Economics verified): headline PCE prices fell month-over-month (the first decline since mid-2022 per PNC's Economic Research note dated 30 July 2026), and core PCE YoY printed at 3.3% (CNBC verified) — the lowest monthly core reading since March 2025. On a year-ago basis, overall PCE inflation was 3.7% in June, down from 4.1% in May (PNC Economics verified). The market's interpretation: the disinflation trend is intact, the Warsh Fed's hawkish-dissent pause was the last gas on the inflation-fear trade, and the multiple-expansion thesis is back as the base case. The 10-year Treasury yield held Thursday's 4.66% area — no further steepener — and the TLT ETF held at $82.10, down only -0.85% on the day, suggesting the bond market is absorbing the negative PCE print as confirmation of lower-for-longer, not as a recession signal. For positioning: this is the cleanest disinflation confirmation since 2024, and it removes the principal macro overhang that the Warsh Fed's hawkish dissent briefly created. The bull case for 7,500+ is back to base case probability.

The third story is the Q2 GDP advance estimate at +1.5% annualized vs +2.5% consensus — a meaningful slowdown from Q1 2026's 2.1%. The contributors were increases in consumer spending, investment, and (per the BEA release) a drop in inventories and government spending. The market's interpretation: the economy is cooling but not contracting — the Goldilocks "soft landing" regime is intact. The 1.5% GDP print combined with the negative-MoM PCE print is the textbook disinflation-with-positive-growth setup: it gives the Fed room to pause (no need to hike into weakness) while supporting corporate revenue (no recession to compress earnings). Current-dollar GDP rose to $32.475 trillion (JEC verified). The 1.5% Q2 print was below consensus by 100 bp — a meaningful miss — but the equity market's reaction (SPX +0.70% Friday, +1.03% WTD) shows that the soft-landing interpretation is dominant. For positioning: the soft-landing regime supports the structural overweight on cyclicals and AI infrastructure (XLI, XLY, XLK) without compressing multiples further. The Q2 GDP print is bullish for equities despite the headline "slowdown" framing.
