VIX at 15.13 — well below the 1-year mean of ~20, in a compressed-vol regime even as the post-ceasefire oil shock hit and the 10Y climbed to 4.74%. The VIX term structure is in steep contango (^VIX9D at ~12.0, ^VIX at 15.13, slope ~-21.0%) — protection is cheap in this regime. The falling-VIX-on-equity-tape-stress paradox is the key signal: the options market is pricing an orderly post-ceasefire absorption, not a panic. With VIX compressed to 15.13, options premiums are cheap: a 10% OTM put on SPY (the ~$685 strike for 90-day expiry, given SPY at ~$765) costs roughly 0.80-0.90% of notional. For investors with significant equity exposure, the current regime supports hedged equity exposure: long defensives (XLV) + real assets (GLD, XLE), underweight rate-sensitive growth-and-multiple expansion (XLK, QQQ), and respect the institutional positioning overlay.

For directional exposure: with VIX compressed and the macro regime in a "post-ceasefire oil shock + Warsh Jackson Hole setup + AI multiple derating" setup, the asymmetry is — upside (7,750-7,850) requires Wednesday PCE to read as in-line-or-softer (core +0.2% MoM or lower), Nvidia Q2 FY27 to re-accelerate guidance, and Warsh Jackson Hole to read as dovish; downside (7,500-7,600) requires hawkish PCE + Nvidia multiple-derating + hawkish Warsh. A bull-put spread on SPY selling the $7,550 put and buying the $7,400 put, 45 DTE, captures the 7,600-7,800 consolidation range while being short vol in a low-VIX regime. The risk: if 30Y breaks above 5.0% sustained, the consolidation range breaks down and the spread gets tested. The institutional positioning overlay (defensive bid + gold breakout + 30Y climb) is consistent with the bull-put spread framework — hedge funds leaning defensively while hedging the rate-sensitive growth cohort.
