As of Sunday, August 2, 2026, the S&P 500 sits at 7,489.72 — Friday's close — after a five-session week that delivered a +1.03% Monday-to-Friday gain but a -0.56% week-over-week decline from the prior Friday's 7,531.86 close. The week's dominant narrative was mega-cap tech earnings validating the AI capex durability thesis — at exactly the moment when the bond market was re-pricing the 10-year yield above 4.70% (TNX hit 4.74% Friday, the highest since November 2023, a +25bp weekly climb). The market is consolidating, not collapsing — and the AI capex thesis has now been validated at the three highest-profile bars of Q2 earnings season (TSMC capex raise, MSFT +15%, AMZN AWS +37% YoY).

The macro setup for the week ahead is dominated by Friday's July Jobs Report (8:30 AM ET) — the most important single data print of the month — with Alphabet's Q2 print Tuesday after-market providing a near-term mega-cap tech read, and the 10-year yield's behavior above 4.70% the dominant macro variable. Weekend Developments: Iran Permit Suspension, Trump Calls Off Strikes, New Tariff Round

The Saturday-Sunday news cycle has been dominated by three storylines: Iran's Hormuz permit suspension, Trump's Saturday call-off of planned strikes, and new tariff escalations.

Iran — the Strait of Hormuz permit system suspended. The most material weekend development was Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority announcement on Thursday July 31 (the same day as the S&P's +0.70% rally) that it would not issue transit permits for vessels to pass through the Strait of Hormuz until the United States ends military operations in the region (per the Institute for the Study of War's August 1 special report, citing the authority's Tasnim news channel). This followed the US Treasury's revocation of a temporary sanctions waiver for Tehran to export oil — one of the few tangible benefits Iran had received from the June 14 memorandum of understanding (per The Guardian's July 8 reporting on the ceasefire breakdown). The ISW August 1 report notes: "The SNSC's statement follows an announcement from Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority on July 31 that it will not issue transit permits for vessels to pass through the strait until the United States ends military operations in the region." The permit system is different from a full physical closure — vessels currently transiting need Iranian government-issued permits — but the suspension means no new permits until US military operations cease, functionally restricting flow. Starboard Maritime Intelligence (per the ISW report, citing the service) noted the implications for commercial shipping. The timing is notable: this happened on the same day as Amazon's record Q2 AWS beat and the S&P's +0.70% close, suggesting the market had not yet fully priced the permit suspension.

Earlier in the day, sources told CNN that Trump spoke with the Saudi crown prince and other regional partners about de-escalation." What Others Are Missing: The 10-Year at 4.74% Is Not the Bear Case — It's the New Normal

The reflexive narrative around the 10-year yield's climb to 4.74% (the highest since November 2023, +25bp on the week) is: "higher rates = multiple compression = equity headwind." That framing is wrong for the current regime — and investors who are underweight equities because of the yield move are missing the actual story.

The 10-year at 4.74% reflects a disinflation economy, not an inflation scare. When the market feared a 2026 rate hike (after the Warsh FOMC's hawkish SEP in June), the 10-year was at 4.45-4.50%. Now, with the disinflation trend confirmed (June PCE negative MoM, core 3.3% YoY), the 10-year has climbed to 4.74%. This is the bond market pricing real growth, not inflation. The 10-year is the market's estimate of nominal GDP growth over the next decade. At 4.74%, the bond market is saying: the US economy will grow at ~2.5-3.0% nominal annually for the next 10 years. That's consistent with 2-2.5% real growth + 2% inflation — the Goldilocks soft-landing scenario. The equity market at 22-23x forward earnings on the S&P is not expensive at 4.74% 10-year if earnings are growing at 10-12% annually. That's a 4.5-5% earnings yield versus a 4.74% risk-free rate. The spread is narrow but positive, and the earnings growth is what drives the multiple, not the rate level.

The VIX collapse to 15.99 is the data point that matters most. The VIX went from a Wednesday intraday high of 20.88 to a Friday close of 15.99 — a -23% intraday decline from peak to close. This is the options market's verdict: the inflation fear and rate-spike fear from Wednesday were absorbed and digested in one session. The Thursday mega-cap tech re-rate (MSFT +15.5%) and the Friday extension (AMZN + after-market) collapsed implied vol. At VIX 15.99, the options market is pricing roughly ±1.1% weekly moves — a calm regime, not a stress regime. Investors who are paying up for downside hedges are overpaying relative to the historical calibration.

The second consecutive weekly SPX loss (-0.56% WoW) is a feature, not a bug. The S&P fell -0.56% from the prior Friday's 7,531.86 to 7,489.72 this Friday — the second consecutive weekly decline. The last time the S&P had two consecutive losing weeks was March 2026 (the tariff-related correction). Two consecutive weekly losses in a structural bull market are normal and healthy: they clear positioning, reset technical indicators, and create entry points for the next move. The prior bull-market analogue was the June 2025 period when the S&P had a similar two-week consolidation before making new highs. The Friday intraday high of 7,512.04 was the highest level since June 18's 7,500.58 close — and the 7,500 area that was resistance in June is now support.

The AMZN AWS +37% YoY is the data point that determines whether the multiple expands. Amazon's AWS at $42.2 billion quarterly revenue ($169B annualized run rate) is a step-change in the AI infrastructure buildout. The hyperscaler capex guidance for 2026 is now in the $300-350 billion range across Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta — and the AI services revenue being generated (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure) is monetizing that capex. The multiple expands when the market sees the revenue from AI infrastructure exceeding the cost of the infrastructure. AWS +37% YoY is that proof. If Alphabet's Tuesday Q2 print confirms Google Cloud at +82% YoY (as reported), the AI infrastructure monetization thesis is validated at all three mega-cap bars of Q2.
